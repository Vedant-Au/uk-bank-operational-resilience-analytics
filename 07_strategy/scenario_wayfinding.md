## Interpretive lens for recommendations

Sminia separates three forms of wayfinding (pp. 1–3, 9–11):

| Mode | What to inspect in this fictional bank | Type of recommendation to evaluate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical coping | Normal service delivery, variations and established workarounds | Maintain and exercise existing response capability; monitor drift from the baseline. |
| Conscious reflecting, a breakdown **in** coping | A needed capability exists elsewhere but is absent from the bank's present practice | Map the capability gap and rehearse adoption, cross-training or supplier coordination under plausible disruptions. |
| Practical solutioning, a breakdown **of** coping | Existing responses no longer work and no ready alternative exists | Test a small, reversible new operating arrangement and learn from observed outcomes before committing broadly. |

Scenarios are sketches, while the organisation's actual path evolves through action and feedback (pp. 2–3, 10–11). Therefore each later recommendation should record: its baseline practice; the observed or simulated breakdown; why it is classified as **in** or **of** coping; an accountable actor; an experiment or exercise; a measurable signal; a review date; and a condition for changing course. This is our **project application** of Sminia's framework, not a tested banking prescription from the paper.

## Evidence and approval gate

No bank-specific recommendation can be inferred from the aggregate Open Banking CSV. The future strategy table must cite simulated internal scenarios as simulated, distinguish them from public contextual evidence, and show uncertainty. Human review should check that exercises or pilots are feasible and align with applicable resilience obligations. A conceptual paper alone cannot establish probabilities, control effectiveness, or regulatory compliance. Do not select a final strategic priority until data quality, EDA and the research question gate are complete.
